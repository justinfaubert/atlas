The Burden of the Salt Coast

Hear this, you who live on the salt coast, you who build your houses on the sand and say, The sea has always been kind to us. Hear it, harbour masters and net menders, and you who count the fish before they are caught.

The tide will turn against you. It will not come in one night, the way the storm comes. It will come the way a debt comes, a little and then a little more, until the day you wake and the water stands in your doorway like a stranger who will not leave.

You have said, Our fathers fished here, and their fathers before them; who will move us? But your fathers did not empty the sea. They took and they left. You take and you do not leave. Therefore the shoals will go out from you, they will go north into the cold water, and you will follow them with empty boats and come home with nothing in your hands.

Woe to the one who builds a wall against the water and calls it safety. Woe to the one who sells the marsh that held the flood. The marsh was your shield, and you sold it for a road. Now the road will be your river.

And yet listen, for this is not the end of the word.

In that day the children will plant the marsh again. They will set the reeds in rows with their own hands, and the old men will laugh at them, and the reeds will grow. Where there was mud there will be grass, and where there was grass there will be birds. The water will come, and the marsh will drink it, and the houses on the hill will stand.

Go up, then, to the hill. Do not wait for the sea to tell you twice. Take your nets and your seed and the names of your dead, and go up. Let the one who has two boats lend one to the one who has none. Let the harbour be shared and not sold.

For a people that remembers the tide will not be swept away by it. A people that measures what it takes will not be measured out. Return to the slow ways, the ways that keep the fish in the sea and the sand on the shore, and you will live on this coast, and your children will live on it after you.

But if you will not hear, the sea will speak more plainly than I can. Its words are cold, and it does not repeat them.

This is the burden of the salt coast. Write it on the harbour wall, above the high water mark, so that those who come after can read it and know that you were told.
